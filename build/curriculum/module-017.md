# Module 17: Papers on artificial intelligence in healthcare

Audience: Healthcare students, practitioners and research learners. Beginners can start with units 1–5; experienced learners can select a relevant topic.
Prerequisites: No advanced statistics assumed. Later units benefit from the question, design, bias and uncertainty foundations in units 1–5.
Need: An algorithm performs well in a dataset. Will it improve care in your setting?
Competence sought: explain appraisal concepts, select a defensible response and articulate uncertainty. Knowledge and reasoning only; no claim of observed clinical performance.
Source: seventh edition (2025), PDF 268–286, printed 249–267.
Estimated effort: 25–40 minutes plus source reading; author estimate, not measured.
Review: 2026-11-06; model author, targeted source comparison. No specialist, psychometric, user or assistive-technology validation. No external update layer.

## Case stages and feedback

Reused stage 1: An AI model performs well retrospectively at its development hospital. A clinic with different patients and a different care pathway wants to use it for triage. Which evidence most directly addresses the clinic's adoption question?

Feedback: Retrospective development performance does not establish clinical usefulness in the new setting. (PDF 277). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.

Reused stage 2: A clinical AI system is modified after deployment. Early evaluation recorded no safety incidents. Which response most appropriately follows from the change and initial evidence?

Feedback: Safety assessment continues as new contexts and changes expose risks not seen initially. (PDF 282). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.
