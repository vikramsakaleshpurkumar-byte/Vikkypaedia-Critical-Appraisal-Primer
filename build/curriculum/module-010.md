# Module 10: Papers that advise you what to do (guidelines)

Audience: Healthcare students, practitioners and research learners. Beginners can start with units 1–5; experienced learners can select a relevant topic.
Prerequisites: No advanced statistics assumed. Later units benefit from the question, design, bias and uncertainty foundations in units 1–5.
Need: A recommendation is only useful if its evidence, development and fit with real care are credible.
Competence sought: explain appraisal concepts, select a defensible response and articulate uncertainty. Knowledge and reasoning only; no claim of observed clinical performance.
Source: seventh edition (2025), PDF 170–182, printed 151–163.
Estimated effort: 25–40 minutes plus source reading; author estimate, not measured.
Review: 2026-11-06; model author, targeted source comparison. No specialist, psychometric, user or assistive-technology validation. No external update layer.

## Actual guided sequence

Opening problem: A recommendation is only useful if its evidence, development and fit with real care are credible.

## Case stages and feedback

Reused stage 1: A guideline panel has strong subject expertise but no methods specialist or patient representative. Its recommendations concern burdensome long-term treatment. Which addition most directly addresses the missing perspectives?

Feedback: Topic expertise does not supply either methodological arbitration or the patient's experience of burden. (PDF 177). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.

Reused stage 2: A guideline favours a product sold by its main funder. Several recommendations are reasonable and supported by trials. Which appraisal response is best justified?

Feedback: Reasonable content does not remove possible influence on framing, option choice or emphasis. (PDF 176). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.
