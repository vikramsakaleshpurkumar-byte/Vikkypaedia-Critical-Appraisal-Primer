# Module 20: Criticisms of evidence-based healthcare

Audience: Healthcare students, practitioners and research learners. Beginners can start with units 1–5; experienced learners can select a relevant topic.
Prerequisites: No advanced statistics assumed. Later units benefit from the question, design, bias and uncertainty foundations in units 1–5.
Need: Whose questions, values and knowledge are represented in the evidence system?
Competence sought: explain appraisal concepts, select a defensible response and articulate uncertainty. Knowledge and reasoning only; no claim of observed clinical performance.
Source: seventh edition (2025), PDF 312–322, printed 293–303.
Estimated effort: 25–40 minutes plus source reading; author estimate, not measured.
Review: 2026-11-06; model author, targeted source comparison. No specialist, psychometric, user or assistive-technology validation. No external update layer.

## Case stages and feedback

Reused stage 1: An organisation prioritises a condition because several RCTs exist, while dismissing a serious unmet need with little trial evidence. Which assumption most directly deserves challenge?

Feedback: Research availability is not a complete map of importance or need. (PDF 314). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.

Reused stage 2: A decision model favours treatment because of its average health gain. A patient values avoiding dependence on daily care and describes burdens not represented in the model. Which response best engages the limitation?

Feedback: An average or utility summary may fail to capture a person's narrative and priorities. (PDF 316). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.
