Audience: Healthcare students, practitioners and research learners. Beginners can start with units 1–5; experienced learners can select a relevant topic.
Prerequisites: No advanced statistics assumed. Later units benefit from the question, design, bias and uncertainty foundations in units 1–5.
Need: A large, impressive study may still answer the wrong question or measure the wrong thing.
Competence sought: explain appraisal concepts, select a defensible response and articulate uncertainty. Knowledge and reasoning only; no claim of observed clinical performance.

## Case stages and feedback

Reused stage 1: An audit counts whether smoking status appears in clinical records and concludes that clinicians asked about smoking in exactly that proportion of consultations. A validation substudy is affordable. Which comparison most directly tests the disputed inference?

Feedback: This checks whether documentation accurately represents the behaviour claimed. (PDF 67). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.

Reused stage 2: A well-conducted trial excludes people over 80 and those with multiple illnesses. Its result is being considered for a frail 86-year-old. Which first appraisal question most directly addresses the present uncertainty?

Feedback: The issue is transfer from the studied population to the intended patient, not an automatic rejection of trial validity. (PDF 66). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.
