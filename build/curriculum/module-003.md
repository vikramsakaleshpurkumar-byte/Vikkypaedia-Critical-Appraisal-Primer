# Module 3: Getting your bearings: what is this paper about?

Audience: Healthcare students, practitioners and research learners. Beginners can start with units 1–5; experienced learners can select a relevant topic.
Prerequisites: No advanced statistics assumed. Later units benefit from the question, design, bias and uncertainty foundations in units 1–5.

## Case stages and feedback

Reused stage 1: Investigators recruit 80 people with a rare disorder and 160 people without it from the same source population. They compare recorded exposure before diagnosis. Which design-specific concern most directly deserves appraisal?

Feedback: Controls need to support a valid comparison with cases; poorly selected controls can distort the exposure association. (PDF 59). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.

Reused stage 2: Before comparing two procedures, researchers find that clinicians define success as technical completion, whereas patients emphasise pain, predictability and privacy. The immediate task is to clarify which outcomes should enter the evaluation. Which study is most directly useful next?

Feedback: This can identify dimensions of success before outcomes are fixed. (PDF 57). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.
