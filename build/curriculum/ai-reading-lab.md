# AI Reading Lab curriculum and evaluation

Audience: students, clinicians and research learners reading healthcare papers. Prerequisite: basic study-design vocabulary; the first five book topics remain recommended. Estimated 5–10 minutes per mission, author estimate. Purpose: use AI for explanation and organisation while independently checking source claims and retaining responsibility.

Competence: knowledge and reasoning, not observed clinical performance. Six missions are open and learner controlled; each has a worked fictional error, a required response before assessment feedback, corrective reasoning and a reusable prompt. Progress records practice, not a certificate. All levels can use the workflow; prompt desk adapts explanation level. No claim of measured learning impact.

All six questions have four distinct choices and per-choice feedback. Six MCQs exceed the five-question minimum for this substantive supplement; eight cards meet the 6–10 card target. Their citations are external source IDs, not invented textbook PDF pages. Earlier source-edition chapter counts and provenance remain intact. Original capstone remains progressive; new missions are independent teaching problems.

Human-first note, source-claim ledger and arithmetic checks are authored recommendations informed by evidence, not findings of a trial. Source categories and limits are visible in Evidence notes. Selected 2025–2026 sources checked 2026-11-06; verify again before a future teaching release. No automatic updates or monitoring configured.

Accessibility implementation: native buttons and form controls, semantic headings, status feedback, no required timing, no reliance on colour alone, reduced-motion styles, preserved keyboard routes. Actual interaction and reflow evidence in ../refresh_checks.json; full screen-reader/WCAG audit and learner evaluation remain unassessed. No personal records are solicited. Reflections, prompts and checks require no external service. Prompt builder fields are temporary; mission progress is browser local.

Review domains: need/outcomes/guided sequence/feedback/version separation met by implementation and source ledger; source/editorial verification model-only; specialist, psychometric, accessibility-conformance and learner-effectiveness review not assessed. Reward design uses fixed, transparent practice completion and best-response counts without claiming dopamine effects. Completion is not mastery.
