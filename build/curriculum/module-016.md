Audience: Healthcare students, practitioners and research learners. Beginners can start with units 1–5; experienced learners can select a relevant topic.
Prerequisites: No advanced statistics assumed. Later units benefit from the question, design, bias and uncertainty foundations in units 1–5.
Need: Similar risks can lead to different reasonable choices when priorities differ.

## Case stages and feedback

Reused stage 1: A clinician accurately explains absolute benefit and harm, then asks a patient to choose without discussing how the options fit the patient's life. The patient asks for help deciding. Which next step best advances shared decision-making?

Feedback: The patient has requested support in relating evidence to personal priorities, not merely more information. (PDF 259). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.

Reused stage 2: A service introduces a patient-reported function scale after surgery. Surgeons' ratings improve, but patient scores remain unchanged. Which first response best addresses the discrepancy?

Feedback: Clinician and patient measures can address different constructs; disagreement needs interpretation rather than automatic hierarchy. (PDF 258). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.
