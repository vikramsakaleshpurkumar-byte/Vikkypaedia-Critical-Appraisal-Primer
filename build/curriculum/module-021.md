# Module 21: Checklists for finding, appraising and implementing evidence

Audience: Healthcare students, practitioners and research learners. Beginners can start with units 1–5; experienced learners can select a relevant topic.
Prerequisites: No advanced statistics assumed. Later units benefit from the question, design, bias and uncertainty foundations in units 1–5.

## Case stages and feedback

Reused stage 1: An appraisal checklist records adequate randomisation, adequate follow-up and a valid outcome. The population differs substantially from the patients who would receive the intervention. Which conclusion should the checklist support?

Feedback: A favourable methods assessment does not itself establish that the result fits the intended patients. (PDF 325). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.

Reused stage 2: A decision aid accurately states the favourable outcome probability but omits the comparison option, uncertainty and important burdens. Which revision most improves its ability to support choice?

Feedback: Accuracy of one favourable number does not provide a balanced decision structure. (PDF 332). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.
