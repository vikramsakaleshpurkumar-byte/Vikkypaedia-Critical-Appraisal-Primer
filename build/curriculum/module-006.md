# Module 6: Papers that report clinical trials of simple interventions

Audience: Healthcare students, practitioners and research learners. Beginners can start with units 1–5; experienced learners can select a relevant topic.
Prerequisites: No advanced statistics assumed. Later units benefit from the question, design, bias and uncertainty foundations in units 1–5.
Need: A new treatment changes a laboratory marker. Has it improved outcomes that patients value?
Competence sought: explain appraisal concepts, select a defensible response and articulate uncertainty. Knowledge and reasoning only; no claim of observed clinical performance.
Source: seventh edition (2025), PDF 101–117, printed 82–98.
Estimated effort: 25–40 minutes plus source reading; author estimate, not measured.

## Case stages and feedback

Reused stage 1: A new drug is compared with a recognised standard. The standard is given at a lower dose than recommended for the question being studied. The new drug performs better on the primary endpoint. Which limitation most directly affects a claim of superiority to adequate standard treatment?

Feedback: The result addresses the comparator regimen actually used, not necessarily an adequately delivered alternative. (PDF 106). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.

Reused stage 2: An efficacy trial of 3,000 participants reports no instances of a serious event thought to occur once per 20,000 exposures. The sponsor calls the event excluded. Which response best matches the trial's information?

Feedback: An efficacy-sized trial can miss very uncommon harms even when its primary efficacy estimate is useful. (PDF 113). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.
