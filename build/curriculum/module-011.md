# Module 11: Papers that estimate what things cost (health economic evaluations)

Audience: Healthcare students, practitioners and research learners. Beginners can start with units 1–5; experienced learners can select a relevant topic.
Prerequisites: No advanced statistics assumed. Later units benefit from the question, design, bias and uncertainty foundations in units 1–5.
Need: The cheapest option and the best value option may differ. Ask whose costs count and what extra benefit is purchased.
Competence sought: explain appraisal concepts, select a defensible response and articulate uncertainty. Knowledge and reasoning only; no claim of observed clinical performance.

## Case stages and feedback

Reused stage 1: A hospital compares two treatments. Their clinical outcomes have not been shown equivalent, but one costs less. The analyst proposes cost-minimisation as the deciding method. Which objection most directly addresses the choice of method?

Feedback: Cost-minimisation assumes equivalent consequences; a cheaper option with different outcomes requires a different analysis. (PDF 184). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.

Reused stage 2: A model favours programme A under its central assumptions. A plausible change in the duration of benefit makes programme B preferable. What is the most useful interpretation for a policymaker?

Feedback: Sensitivity analysis has identified a decision-relevant uncertainty, which should be explicit in the recommendation. (PDF 194). Response required before feedback; Continue before next stage.
