# AI reading update — source and design record

Checked: 2026-11-06. Added to the existing course without modifying source-edition chapter prose. Selected primary studies, official methodological/editorial guidance and official announcements; not an exhaustive review. Model synthesis, no independent specialist validation.

The new six-mission curriculum teaches human-supervised reading, claim checking, numeric interpretation, methods appraisal, tool-category selection and accountability. Original prompts, fictional excerpts and challenges are teaching designs, not reproduced empirical examples or validated interventions. No live AI service is connected.

## Sources actually consulted

- [Peters & Chin-Yee — Generalization bias in LLM summarization of scientific research](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.241776) — 2025-04; Primary empirical study. The tested models sometimes broadened conclusions by dropping qualifiers. Results concern the models, prompts and material studied; they are not a current ranking of every AI system.
- [Asai et al. — Synthesizing scientific literature with retrieval-augmented language models](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12935541/) — 2026-02-04; Primary system and benchmark evaluation. Retrieval-supported synthesis improved benchmark performance, yet retrieval could miss relevant papers and answers could contain unsupported information. Evaluation preferences also had limitations. This does not establish a replacement for a systematic review.
- [Gartlehner et al. — Responsible Integration of AI in Rapid Reviews](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12644243/) — 2025-11-24; Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group position statement. For Cochrane rapid reviews, AI should assist under human oversight, with justified use, verified outputs and transparent reporting of tools, model versions and prompts. This is methodological guidance, not an accuracy trial.
- [ICMJE — Use of AI by Authors](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-authors.html) — Current page checked 2026-11-06; Editorial recommendations. Humans retain responsibility for submitted content. Authors should disclose relevant AI use, check accuracy and attribution, and cite original sources rather than treating generated text as primary evidence.
- [ICMJE — Editors’ Role in Ensuring Responsible Use of AI](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-editors.html) — Current page checked 2026-11-06; Editorial recommendations. Submitted manuscripts are confidential. Editors and reviewers should not upload them to AI systems where confidentiality cannot be assured without explicit author permission; journal policies also apply.
- [WHO — Artificial intelligence-related health research: ethics review and oversight](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240124073) — 2026-07-21; Official report overview. The report examines oversight of research using AI, research about AI, and AI-related health data science, including fairness and power imbalances. Only the public overview was used here; no claim of full-report appraisal.
- [Cochrane launches independent study of AI tools for evidence synthesis](https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/cochrane-launches-innovative-study-assess-ai-tools-evidence-synthesis) — 2026-02-19; Official research announcement. Cochrane described plans for independent, prospective evaluation because existing assessments have limitations. An announced evaluation is not a result or endorsement of a specific product.

## Access and interpretation limits

Royal Society publisher access was restricted; the journal abstract and author-university repository record were available. The OpenScholar primary article's detailed results/limitations were returned through the indexed PMC full-text record; a later direct open encountered a browser challenge. No full-text claims are made about the WHO report beyond its official overview. ICMJE live pages and the Cochrane rapid-review position statement were read directly. The Cochrane 2026 study announcement is not treated as completed results. A recent BMJ reporting review was found but not used for substantive teaching because publisher access was restricted.

No product ranking, vendor pricing, efficacy guarantee, quantified time-saving claim or claim of exhaustive current coverage. No dopamine or neurological effect is measured or claimed. The interface uses transparent progress, fixed feedback, optional retries and clear stopping points; no streak penalties, artificial scarcity, timers or remote telemetry.

## Supplement schema and counts

AI_LAB has reviewed date, sources, six missions and eight cards. Each mission has an outcome, teaching sequence, labelled fictional source/output, one five-option authored decision, keyed answer, feedback for every alternative, prompt, human checks and source IDs. The six decisions are distinct from the 110 book-based questions. The eight cards are distinct from the 132 book-based cards. Combined exports: 140 cards; total authored assessment bank: 116 decisions plus the existing three capstone decisions. Existing 22 topic exercises reuse 44 book questions and are not counted twice.

Local storage key is the existing course key plus _ai_lab; old card/guided keys are preserved. Mission records contain practised, best response reached and most recent answer. Prompt-builder fields are temporary and not stored or transmitted. The app has no API integration and makes no AI calls.
