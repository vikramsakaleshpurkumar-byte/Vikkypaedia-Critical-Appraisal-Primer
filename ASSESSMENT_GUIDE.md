# Using the rebuilt Assessment Studio — version 2

The active bank contains 116 rewritten decisions: 70 best-of-five, 23 assertion–reason and 23 data-interpretation items. The 110 book questions still provide five items per topic across all 22 chapters/appendices; six AI-update items are separate. The progressive capstone has three additional rewritten best-of-five decisions. The two-stage topic exercises reuse items from the book bank and do not increase the count.

All scenarios and numbers are authored teaching examples. The intended cognitive demand is application, interpretation and appraisal; actual difficulty and discrimination have not been measured with learners.

## Best of five

Select the single best response to the specific lead-in under the stated facts. Several choices may describe useful activities, but one should most directly solve the problem asked. Before reading the options, identify the decision and its crucial condition. Then explain why the closest competing response is less suitable here.

## Assertion–reason

Judge A, judge R, then evaluate the proposed explanatory link. The conventional response order is fixed:

A. Both are true and R correctly explains A.
B. Both are true but R does not correctly explain A.
C. A is true and R is false.
D. A is false and R is true.
E. Both are false.

Two true statements are not necessarily an explanation. These items are included at the user's request; they are not claimed to be a current NBME examination format or to have been psychometrically validated.

## Data interpretation

Interpret the supplied evidence rather than recognising a definition. Some items use event rates, confidence intervals, likelihood ratios or costs; others ask what a reported study process actually supports. Keep denominators, outcome, time horizon, comparison and assumptions explicit. Calculations use the supplied hypothetical values, not prescribing recommendations.

## Practice and test modes

Choose an option, optionally record confidence and your deciding reason, then Lock answer. Practice mode shows the best-response rationale and selected-distractor feedback after locking. Expand Compare all five alternatives to examine the other choices and follow the source link.

Test mode withholds correctness, sources and explanations until Submit test block. It permits submission with unanswered items and reports the full-block denominator separately from answered-item accuracy. This is an offline self-assessment mode, not a secure or proctored examination.

Review previous mistakes prioritises high-confidence errors in the latest version-2 attempt. Flagging records an item for further review. Written explanations and confidence are learner records, not automatically graded proof of reasoning. Export this attempt creates a JSON record under learner control. No response data are sent elsewhere.

Old AI-question success is not transferred to the rewritten questions: earlier activity is retained separately and the new decisions start unanswered. Book card and guided completion remain intact. Same-item retries are practice and cannot establish independent learning gain.

## How the items were reviewed

The rewrite removed deliberately unrelated distractors and replaced them with competing design choices, incomplete interpretations and specific calculation errors. Keys were checked for option-length cues and positional patterns. Source references, arithmetic examples and all five feedback entries were reviewed; the source reader remains authoritative.

Selected principles for the BOFs—focused lead-ins, plausible options and removal of avoidable clues—were informed by the [NBME Item-Writing Guide](https://www.nbme.org/sites/default/files/2021-02/NBME_Item%20Writing%20Guide_R_6.pdf). This is an internal editorial application of guidance, not NBME review, endorsement or certification.

Model source/editorial checking is complete. Independent subject-expert best-answer review, learner piloting, distractor selection rates and item discrimination remain unassessed. A five-option format alone does not establish quality or make every question difficult; foundational and advanced reasoning tasks both remain in scope.
