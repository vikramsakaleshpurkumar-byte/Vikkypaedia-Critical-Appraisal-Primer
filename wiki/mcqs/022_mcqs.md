## q-022-1 · Best of five

Over one year, 15 of 100 controls and 10 of 100 treated participants have an undesirable event. Which statement correctly compares absolute and relative effects?

A. ARR is 10 percentage points and relative risk is 1.5
B. ARR is 15 percentage points and relative risk is 0.50
C. ARR is 5 percentage points and relative risk is about 0.67
D. ARR is 5% and relative risk reduction is 5%
E. ARR is 33 percentage points and relative risk is about 0.67

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: C. ARR is 5 percentage points and relative risk is about 0.67

The risk difference is 0.15 − 0.10; relative risk is 0.10/0.15.

## q-022-5 · Data interpretation

Trial X has 20 events among 200 controls and 10 among 100 treated participants. Trial Y has 20 among 100 controls and 10 among 100 treated participants. Follow-up and endpoints match. Which comparison of ARR is correct?

A. X has ARR 0; Y has ARR 0.10
B. Both have ARR 0 because the treated counts are identical
C. Both have ARR 0.10 because each has ten fewer treated events
D. X has ARR 0.05; Y has ARR 0.10
E. X has ARR 0.10; Y has ARR 0.20

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: A. X has ARR 0; Y has ARR 0.10

X's risks are both 0.10; Y's are 0.20 and 0.10. Counts require their own denominators.

**A.** X's risks are both 0.10; Y's are 0.20 and 0.10. Counts require their own denominators. (PDF 335)

**B.** The comparison depends on control and treated risks, not treated counts alone. (PDF 335)

**C.** Equal count differences do not imply equal risk differences when denominators differ. (PDF 335)

**D.** X's control risk is 0.10, not 0.15. (PDF 335)

**E.** These are not the differences between the stated event proportions. (PDF 335)
