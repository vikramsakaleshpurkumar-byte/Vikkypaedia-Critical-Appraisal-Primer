## q-012-1 · Best of five

Researchers seek to explain why some patients find a service accessible and others avoid it. They deliberately recruit contrasting users and non-users, rather than a random sample of current attenders. Which appraisal criterion best fits this choice?

A. Whether non-users were excluded to reduce variation in the interviews
B. Whether the sample size guarantees a narrow prevalence interval
C. Whether the selected experiences can illuminate the explanatory question
D. Whether every service user had the same probability of selection
E. Whether the sample reproduces the age distribution of current attenders exactly

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: C. Whether the selected experiences can illuminate the explanatory question

Purposive selection can appropriately explore relevant contrasts; it is not intended to estimate a population average by itself.

**A.** Their contrasting experience is part of the intended explanation. (PDF 205)

**B.** The study is not designed primarily to estimate prevalence. (PDF 205)

**C.** Purposive selection can appropriately explore relevant contrasts; it is not intended to estimate a population average by itself. (PDF 205)

**D.** That is central to a probability survey, not necessarily this qualitative aim. (PDF 205)

**E.** Matching current attenders can exclude the non-user perspective central to the question. (PDF 205)

## q-012-2 · Best of five

A qualitative team develops a theory from interviews. Several accounts contradict it. Which analytic response most strengthens the explanatory work?

A. Examine the contradictory accounts and revise or qualify the explanation
B. Add more quotations from the most articulate supporting participants
C. Convert the supporting quotations into a percentage without revisiting the theory
D. Remove the accounts if the majority support the theory
E. Report that the software assigned the same theme to every interview

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: A. Examine the contradictory accounts and revise or qualify the explanation

Negative cases test the account's scope and help prevent selective use of supporting quotations.

**A.** Negative cases test the account's scope and help prevent selective use of supporting quotations. (PDF 207)

**B.** Rich quotations can still produce a selectively supported explanation. (PDF 207)

**C.** Quantifying selected material does not test the explanation against negative cases. (PDF 207)

**D.** Majority frequency does not justify suppressing contrary evidence. (PDF 207)

**E.** A software label does not resolve contradictory meaning. (PDF 207)

## q-012-5 · Data interpretation

D. Which operating system ran the qualitative software
