## q-005-1 · Best of five

A trial estimates a risk ratio of 0.86 with a 95% confidence interval of 0.64–1.16. A reviewer calls it evidence of no benefit. What is the most defensible correction?

A. The interval should be omitted because it contains the no-effect value
B. The interval includes clinically relevant benefit as well as no effect
C. The upper limit establishes that the treatment increases risk
D. The trial establishes that a 14% relative reduction is clinically important
E. The lower limit should replace the point estimate for decision-making

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: B. The interval includes clinically relevant benefit as well as no effect

The estimate remains imprecise; a nonsignificant result has not established no important benefit.

**A.** Including no effect is precisely why the uncertainty needs interpretation. (PDF 95)

**B.** The estimate remains imprecise; a nonsignificant result has not established no important benefit. (PDF 95)

**C.** An interval limit is not the single established treatment effect. (PDF 95)

**D.** The point estimate does not itself establish the clinical importance or certainty of that reduction. (PDF 95)

**E.** A favourable compatible value is not the best estimate merely because it is clinically attractive. (PDF 95)

## q-005-5 · Data interpretation

Two programmes each reduce relative event risk by 25% over one year. Baseline risk is 20% in population X and 4% in population Y. Assuming the stated relative effect applies in both populations, which absolute-risk comparison is correct?

A. ARR is 4 percentage points in X and 20 points in Y
B. ARR is 25 percentage points in both populations
C. ARR is 5 percentage points in X and 1 point in Y
D. ARR is 1 percentage point in X and 5 points in Y
E. ARR is 16 percentage points in X and 3 points in Y

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: C. ARR is 5 percentage points in X and 1 point in Y

Treated risks are 15% and 3%; subtract each from its own baseline risk.
