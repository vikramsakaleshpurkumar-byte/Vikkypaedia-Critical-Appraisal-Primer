## q-008-1 · Best of five

A new diagnostic test is evaluated using only clear-cut severe cases and healthy volunteers. The intended clinic population mostly has borderline symptoms and competing diagnoses. Which change most directly improves the relevance of the validation?

A. Use a narrower confidence interval as the main inclusion criterion

C. Choose a higher positive threshold using the same extreme groups
D. Recruit a representative spectrum of people undergoing the clinical work-up

Best response: D. Recruit a representative spectrum of people undergoing the clinical work-up

## q-008-5 · Data interpretation

Before testing, disease probability is 20%. The likelihood ratio for the observed result is 4. Assuming this LR applies to the patient, what is the post-test probability?

A. 80%
B. 20%
C. 50%
D. 25%
E. 24%

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: C. 50%

Pre-test odds are 0.20/0.80 = 0.25; multiplying by 4 gives odds 1, hence probability 1/2.

**A.** This multiplies probability by the LR instead of updating odds. (PDF 139)

**B.** This leaves the probability unchanged despite an informative LR. (PDF 139)

**C.** Pre-test odds are 0.20/0.80 = 0.25; multiplying by 4 gives odds 1, hence probability 1/2. (PDF 139)

**D.** This is the pre-test odds expressed as a percentage, not the post-test probability. (PDF 139)

**E.** This adds four percentage points to 20% rather than updating odds by the likelihood ratio. (PDF 139)
