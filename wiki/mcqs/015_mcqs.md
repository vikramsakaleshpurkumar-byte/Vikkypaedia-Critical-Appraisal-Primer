## q-015-1 · Best of five

A genome-wide study identifies a SNP associated with a disease. The SNP lies near a biologically plausible gene, but no functional work has tested the variant. Which next finding most directly strengthens a causal interpretation of this variant itself?

A. The same SNP having a smaller p-value in the original sample
B. The disease having a high heritability estimate in another population
C. The associated region containing several additional correlated SNPs
D. The SNP appearing in a more widely used genotyping panel

## q-015-5 · Data interpretation

A variant is associated with a disease at OR 1.15. A report tells a carrier that the disease is likely to develop. Baseline risk and individual predictive performance are not supplied. Which missing information most directly limits that individual prediction?

A. The relative frequency of the variant among all laboratory samples
B. Absolute risk and validated predictive performance in the relevant population
C. Whether the association remains significant after multiple-testing correction
D. Whether the carrier has other SNPs measured by the same commercial panel
E. Whether the association was observed using two genotyping platforms

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: B. Absolute risk and validated predictive performance in the relevant population

A modest association measure alone does not quantify the carrier's chance of developing disease.
