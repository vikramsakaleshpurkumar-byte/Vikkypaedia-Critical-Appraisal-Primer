## q-011-1 · Best of five

A hospital compares two treatments. Their clinical outcomes have not been shown equivalent, but one costs less. The analyst proposes cost-minimisation as the deciding method. Which objection most directly addresses the choice of method?

A. The cheaper treatment must have a higher cost per successful outcome
B. A randomised trial cannot supply economic information
C. The analysis must exclude all patient-incurred costs
D. The comparison should be based on average purchase price alone
E. Outcome equivalence has not been established for a cost-only comparison

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: E. Outcome equivalence has not been established for a cost-only comparison

Cost-minimisation assumes equivalent consequences; a cheaper option with different outcomes requires a different analysis.

**B.** Economic evaluations may be conducted alongside trials. (PDF 184)

**C.** Cost inclusion depends on perspective, not on a requirement to omit patient costs. (PDF 184)

## q-011-5 · Data interpretation

A new programme costs £1,200 per patient and produces 0.80 QALY; usual care costs £1,000 and produces 0.75 QALY over the same horizon. What is the incremental cost per additional QALY?

A. £4,000
B. £24,000
C. £1,333
D. £1,500
E. £200

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: A. £4,000

The additional cost is £200 and additional effect 0.05 QALY; 200/0.05 = 4,000.

**A.** The additional cost is £200 and additional effect 0.05 QALY; 200/0.05 = 4,000. (PDF 194)

**B.** This divides the total new-programme cost by the incremental effect rather than the incremental cost. (PDF 194)

**C.** This approximates usual care's average ratio and does not compare the differences. (PDF 194)

**D.** This is the new programme's average cost per QALY, not its incremental ratio. (PDF 194)

**E.** This is the cost difference without dividing by the effect difference. (PDF 194)
