## q-004-5 · Data interpretation

A trial was planned to detect a large benefit. Its result is not statistically significant, but the interval remains compatible with a smaller benefit clinicians would still value. Which conclusion best fits the information?

A. The result rules out benefit because the planned sample was reached
B. The trial demonstrates equivalence at the smaller benefit threshold
C. The smaller benefit should be treated as the best estimate
D. The primary endpoint should be replaced by the strongest secondary endpoint
E. The result is inconclusive about the smaller clinically important benefit

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: E. The result is inconclusive about the smaller clinically important benefit

The study has not excluded the decision-relevant benefit; failure to detect the larger target is insufficient.

**A.** Achieving the planned size does not guarantee precision for a different, smaller effect. (PDF 74)

**B.** Equivalence requires appropriate margins and evidence excluding relevant differences. (PDF 74)

**C.** Compatibility with a value does not make it the point estimate. (PDF 74)

**D.** Changing endpoints does not resolve uncertainty in the original estimate. (PDF 74)

**E.** The study has not excluded the decision-relevant benefit; failure to detect the larger target is insufficient. (PDF 74)
