## q-006-1 · Best of five

A new drug is compared with a recognised standard. The standard is given at a lower dose than recommended for the question being studied. The new drug performs better on the primary endpoint. Which limitation most directly affects a claim of superiority to adequate standard treatment?

A. The trial should report only absolute rather than relative effects
B. The trial should be interpreted as a placebo-controlled comparison
C. The new drug's endpoint should be converted to a standardised effect size
D. The standard arm's baseline risk should be used as an external control
E. The study contrast differs from the intended clinical contrast

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: E. The study contrast differs from the intended clinical contrast

The result addresses the comparator regimen actually used, not necessarily an adequately delivered alternative.

**A.** Absolute presentation improves interpretation but leaves the treatment contrast unchanged. (PDF 106)

**B.** A weak active regimen is not equivalent to a properly specified placebo contrast. (PDF 106)

**C.** A different scale does not repair the comparator regimen. (PDF 106)

**D.** Reusing that arm does not make its dose appropriate. (PDF 106)

## q-006-2 · Best of five

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

## q-006-5 · Data interpretation

A paper reports a significant 12% biomarker reduction, no difference in recorded symptoms and no mortality follow-up. Which outcome claim can be made directly from these results?

A. The treatment reduced mortality by the same relative percentage
B. The treatment had no patient benefit because symptoms were unchanged
C. The treatment is clinically equivalent because symptoms did not differ
D. The treatment changed the biomarker; longer survival is not established
E. The treatment improved survival because the biomarker is prognostic

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Best response: D. The treatment changed the biomarker; longer survival is not established

The reported measures support a marker effect, not an unmeasured survival outcome.

**A.** Transferring the biomarker percentage to mortality is unjustified. (PDF 107)

**B.** Other outcomes and uncertainty remain; the data do not support this broad negative conclusion. (PDF 107)

**C.** A nonsignificant symptom comparison does not establish equivalence. (PDF 107)

**D.** The reported measures support a marker effect, not an unmeasured survival outcome. (PDF 107)

**E.** Prognostic relevance does not establish a treatment-mediated survival benefit. (PDF 107)
