<details><summary>What distinguishes a randomised controlled trial?</summary>

Participants are allocated by a random process to comparison groups and followed for prespecified outcomes.

</details>

<details><summary>Does having a control group prove a study was randomised?</summary>

No. Allocation can be non-random even when an intervention and control group are compared.

</details>

<details><summary>Why might qualitative methods be needed when evaluating care?</summary>

Patients may value aspects of an experience that standard numerical outcomes do not capture.

</details>

<details><summary>What is the starting point of a case-control study?</summary>

Select people with a condition and suitable controls, then investigate previous exposures.

</details>

<details><summary>Why is an RCT not the best design for every question?</summary>

RCTs are particularly suited to intervention questions; other questions require other designs or methods.
