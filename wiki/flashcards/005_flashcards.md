<details><summary>Why is a two-sided test generally appropriate?</summary>

An intervention can plausibly improve or worsen the outcome unless a one-direction assumption is convincingly justified.

</details>

<details><summary>Why is a non-significant p-value not proof of no difference?</summary>

The study may lack enough information to detect a difference; significance alone cannot distinguish this from no effect.

</details>

<details><summary>What do confidence intervals add to a point estimate?</summary>

They describe precision and the range of effect sizes compatible with the estimate under the model.

<details><summary>Why express benefit in absolute as well as relative terms?</summary>

Relative reductions can obscure baseline risk; absolute effects and numbers needed to treat put benefit into context.
