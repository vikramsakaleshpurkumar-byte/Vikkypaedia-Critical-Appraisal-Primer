Browsing to keep current, answering a focused clinical question, and comprehensively surveying a research topic.

<details><summary>When can a focused clinical search reasonably stop?</summary>

When trustworthy, relevant information has been found; this is different from an exhaustive research review.

</details>

<details><summary>Why is a single simple database query inadequate for a comprehensive review?</summary>

A review needs systematic searching of relevant databases and citation chaining, usually with an information professional.

PDF p. 36
</details>

<details><summary>What can a systematic review inherit from its primary studies?</summary>

Their methodological or design flaws; synthesis does not automatically remove those flaws.

PDF p. 38
</details>

<details><summary>How can secondary sources help locate primary research?</summary>

Their references and hyperlinks provide routes to relevant original studies.

PDF p. 42
</details>

<details><summary>What is the attraction of a federated search engine?</summary>

It searches several types of evidence resource at once; search convenience still does not establish evidence quality.
