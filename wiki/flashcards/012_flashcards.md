<details><summary>What kinds of material can qualitative research analyse?</summary>

Interviews, observations, documents and other meaning-rich data to develop explanations and understanding.

</details>

<details><summary>What is reflexivity?</summary>

Explicit consideration of how the researcher's perspective and role may shape data collection, interpretation and claims.

PDF p. 201
</details>

<details><summary>What is triangulation?</summary>

Bringing more than one method or perspective to bear on a question to enrich and test interpretation.

PDF p. 201
</details>

<details><summary>What is progressive focusing?</summary>

Refining the research question and methods as emerging findings clarify what needs investigation.

PDF p. 202
</details>

<details><summary>Why use purposive sampling?</summary>

To seek relevant and contrasting experiences that illuminate the question, rather than merely estimate an average population value.

PDF p. 205
</details>

<details><summary>Why actively examine contradictory or negative cases?</summary>

A defensible analysis must test and refine explanations rather than cherry-pick appealing quotations.

</details>
