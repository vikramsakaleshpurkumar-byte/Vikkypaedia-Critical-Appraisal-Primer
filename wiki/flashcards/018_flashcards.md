<details><summary>What is mechanistic evidence?</summary>

Evidence about the causal process through which an intervention or exposure produces an effect.

PDF p. 287
</details>

<details><summary>How can mechanisms explain a failed feeding programme?</summary>

Food may not be eaten, absorbed or delivered as expected; local biological and social pathways can block effects.

PDF p. 288
</details>

<details><summary>Are mechanistic studies equally strong?</summary>

No. Their quality and contribution to a coherent causal explanation need appraisal; a simple hierarchy is insufficient.

PDF p. 289
</details>

<details><summary>How do mechanisms and probabilistic evidence contribute together?</summary>

They address complementary aspects of causal inference, including observed associations and causal pathways.

PDF p. 290
</details>

<details><summary>What does EBM+ add to appraisal?</summary>

Consider mechanistic alongside probabilistic evidence rather than treating either as a universal substitute.

PDF p. 291
</details>

<details><summary>Why distinguish advice from actual exposure?</summary>

If advice does not change behaviour, its effect does not establish the effect of consistently receiving the intervention.
