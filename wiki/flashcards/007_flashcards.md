<details><summary>Why develop and pilot a complex intervention before definitive testing?</summary>

To refine components and identify acceptability, feasibility and operational problems.

</details>

<details><summary>What distinguishes core from adaptable components?</summary>

Core elements should remain consistent; non-core elements can be adapted to local context based on development work.

</details>

<details><summary>Why describe the control package in detail?</summary>

Otherwise differences may arise from co-interventions, such as human contact, rather than the component being credited.

PDF p. 122
</details>

<details><summary>What should a theory of action explain?</summary>

How the intervention's components are expected to produce the intended outcomes.

PDF p. 122
</details>

<details><summary>What does a process evaluation investigate?</summary>

How implementation occurred, fidelity to the plan, practical barriers and participant or staff experiences.

PDF p. 124
</details>

<details><summary>Why does one negative complex-intervention trial not refute every adaptation?</summary>

The package may be poorly optimised or implemented, and effects may depend on setting and component delivery.
