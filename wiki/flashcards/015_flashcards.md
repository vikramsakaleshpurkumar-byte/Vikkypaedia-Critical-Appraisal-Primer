<details><summary>What is a genome-wide association study?</summary>

A large observational study comparing genomic variants in relation to traits or disease, often using cohort or case-control designs.

PDF p. 241
</details>

<details><summary>Why is a disease-associated SNP not automatically causal?</summary>

It can mark an associated genomic region without itself being the causal variant; biological plausibility needs further investigation.

PDF p. 243
</details>

<details><summary>Does a risk-associated variant predict inevitable disease?</summary>

No. Many associations are weak and probabilistic, so an association is not a deterministic individual prediction.

PDF p. 244
</details>

<details><summary>Why do observational quality checks still matter in GWAS?</summary>

Case and control selection, population differences, confounding and measurement can still distort high-technology studies.

PDF p. 250
</details>

<details><summary>Why define phenotypes precisely?</summary>

Inconsistent or unclear disease and trait definitions can undermine genetic association analyses.

PDF p. 251
</details>

<details><summary>What is the winner&#x27;s curse?</summary>

Initially detected associations can have inflated effect estimates because larger apparent effects more readily cross significance thresholds.

</details>
