Decisions about individual patients, with benefits and harms interpreted in their clinical context.

<details><summary>What are the five broad steps of evidence-based practice?</summary>

Formulate an answerable question; find evidence; appraise validity and usefulness; implement; evaluate performance.

</details>

<details><summary>Why is critical appraisal alone insufficient?</summary>

The question and source must fit the problem, and valid findings must be implemented and evaluated.

</details>

<details><summary>What four components make a focused intervention question?</summary>

Population, intervention, comparison and outcomes that matter; the manoeuvre may instead be an exposure or test.

PDF p. 31
</details>

<details><summary>Why can a factual essay fail to solve a patient&#x27;s problem?</summary>

It may describe a condition without isolating the practical decision and information the patient needs.

</details>

<details><summary>Why must patient preferences enter question formulation?</summary>

The relevant outcome and acceptable trade-offs depend on the particular patient's circumstances and values.
