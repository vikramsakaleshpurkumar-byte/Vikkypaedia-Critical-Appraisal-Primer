<details><summary>When is cost-minimisation analysis appropriate?</summary>

When compared interventions have equivalent outcomes; otherwise simply choosing the cheapest can mislead.

<details><summary>Why use economic modelling in addition to a single trial?</summary>

A model can compare more options and longer-term consequences, but its assumptions and input evidence need appraisal.

<details><summary>What does a quality-adjusted life-year combine?</summary>

Time lived and a preference-based quality weight for that health state; the valuation method and whose values are used matter.

</details>

<details><summary>What does an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio compare?</summary>

The difference in costs divided by the difference in effects between alternatives, rather than their separate average ratios.

</details>

<details><summary>Why state an economic evaluation&#x27;s perspective?</summary>

It determines whose costs and benefits are included, such as the health service, patient or society.

To see whether reasonable changes in uncertain assumptions or inputs change the economic conclusion.
