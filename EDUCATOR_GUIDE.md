# Teaching with Evidence Skills

Use the original source and course together. Before teaching, review questions and explanations for your learner level. Before distributing a source-inclusive copy, confirm the necessary source permissions. This is not an accredited course or validated exam.

## Four adaptable journal-club sessions

|Session|Preparation|In-session activity|Evidence of learning|
|---|---|---|---|
|Ask and find|Units 1–3 Essentials|Turn a real information need into a focused question; justify search completeness and design choice.|Written question and explanation of why its search/design fits.|
|Judge methods and results|Units 4–5 and appendix 2|Find a bias threat; calculate a fictional absolute benefit; distinguish precision from importance.|A justified appraisal paragraph and worked calculation.|
|Match appraisal to design|Choose units 6–15, 17–19 relevant to the group|Use an actual paper supplied by the educator; compare judgments before checking the toolkit.|Source-cited critique addressing strengths, limits and uncertainty.|
|Apply with people and context|Units 16, 20 and the progressive capstone|Discuss how burden, values and setting change the decision; critique an overly certain conclusion.|A supported decision and an explicit list of unresolved questions.|

Plan 45–60 minutes per discussion as an adjustable teaching estimate. Beginners may need more time and a worked example; advanced learners can use Deep Dive and challenge assumptions in the source. The two-stage topic exercises and quiz studio share items; they cannot serve as independent pre/post examinations.

## Facilitation prompts

Ask: What exactly was studied? What did the investigators actually do? What else could explain this result? How large and how uncertain is the effect? Who is missing from the study? What matters to this patient or setting? What evidence would change your view?

Invite a brief private answer before group discussion. Ask learners to justify rejected alternatives, not only the keyed choice. Treat disagreement as a prompt to examine assumptions. Use original full-page views for complex tables or figures rather than relying on flattened text.

## Follow-up and evaluation

Use the six cards per topic the next day and about a week later. Suggested intervals are not an automated schedule. Have learners record clarity, relevance, workload and remaining questions in their local reflection. Obtain consent and use a separately agreed process if collecting reflections; this course sends no data to an educator. Assess real clinical performance through appropriate observed methods rather than equating quiz accuracy with competence.


## Optional human–AI paper-reading workshop

Use AI Reading Lab after core question/design teaching. Invite learners to write their own initial judgement, then compare the labelled flawed output with the fictional source. Ask them to identify the overclaim, restore the denominator/time horizon and document unresolved methods. Finish by adapting a prompt and explaining which outputs require human checks. The six mission decisions and eight cards are new; all examples are authored, not real model performance observations.

For a live tool demonstration, use a permitted published source and an institutionally approved service. The course itself makes no AI calls. Record the tool/version, task, supplied material and human corrections. Do not substitute a prompted summary for an observed appraisal performance.
